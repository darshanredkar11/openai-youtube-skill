# Niche research and scoring rubric

Use this rubric to compare candidate niches consistently. Score each dimension from 1–5, multiply by its weight, and convert to a 100-point total. Scores organise judgement; they are not measured probabilities.

| Dimension | Weight | What earns a high score |
|---|---:|---|
| Audience demand | 20 | Multiple current signals that a definable audience actively seeks answers or entertainment in this topic |
| Monetisation fit | 20 | Clear buyer intent and credible products, services, sponsors, affiliates or lead-generation paths |
| Audience value / intent | 15 | The viewer has a meaningful problem, budget, urgency or repeat need |
| Competitive headroom | 15 | Evidence of underserved questions, stale coverage, weak explanations or smaller channels outperforming their baseline |
| Differentiation | 10 | A defensible angle based on skill, access, region, language, testing, reporting or experience |
| Topic durability and supply | 10 | At least 30 useful follow-on topics and a meaningful evergreen component |
| Production feasibility | 10 | Can be made reliably with available time, skills, budget and legitimate assets |

Calculation: `total = Σ(weight × score/5)`.

## Evidence to collect

For every candidate, capture:
- Viewer and problem/desire in one sentence.
- Five or more relevant channels where available.
- Recent video examples with URL, publication date, views and duration.
- Channel baseline and outliers; collect at least four videos per channel before using a median.
- Visible audience questions or repeated search intents.
- Existing products/services and plausible monetisation routes.
- What competitors consistently fail to explain or demonstrate.
- Estimated production hours and tools needed.
- Copyright, privacy, factual, medical/financial/legal or platform-policy risks.

Prefer dated, primary or directly observable evidence. A view count is a snapshot, not proof of demand over time. Public data does not reveal CTR, retention, RPM or conversion.

## Revenue model discipline

When user-provided or credible cited inputs exist, calculate scenarios explicitly:
- Ad revenue estimate: `monetised views ÷ 1,000 × RPM`.
- Affiliate revenue estimate: `qualified clicks × conversion rate × commission`.
- Product/service revenue estimate: `qualified leads × close rate × gross contribution`.

Show input provenance and low/base/high assumptions. Do not present a scenario as a forecast. Do not quote a generic niche RPM as fact; geography, format, seasonality, advertiser demand and audience mix can change the result substantially.

## Red flags / hard gates

A high numeric score does not rescue a niche where:
- Success depends on reusing others' footage, scripts, music or trademarks without permission.
- The only proposed advantage is mass-producing near-identical AI videos.
- Claims cannot be checked or require expertise the creator does not have.
- There is no meaningful path to a differentiated channel promise.
- Production is too costly or complex to sustain.
- Monetisation depends on a speculative RPM with no other buyer-intent path.

## Decision

Recommend one primary niche and one backup. State the strongest evidence, the biggest unknown, the cheapest test, and the result that would make you abandon or change the idea.
